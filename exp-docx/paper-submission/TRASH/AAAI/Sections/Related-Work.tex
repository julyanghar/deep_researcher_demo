\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Agent workload studies.}
Serving-oriented studies of agent workloads are recent. TraceLab characterizes coding-agent traces at the systems level and discards message text during anonymization, so content overlap cannot be recovered from its data~\citep{tracelab}. Similar systems-level studies cover ReAct-style agents~\citep{agentic-workload-char} and memory-augmented agents~\citep{agent-memory}. Content overlap has been measured, but at other layers: term adoption in agentic search-query logs~\citep{agentic-search-wild}, redundancy of final reports under LLM judges~\citep{reporteval}, and byte-exact duplication inside single-turn RAG prompts~\citep{byte-exact-dedup}. Our copy metrics descend from extractiveness measures in summarization~\citep{grusky2018,copy-paste-rag}. None of these reaches the LLM-call layer of a multi-agent pipeline with the text intact, which is where content recycling lives and where we measure it.

\paragraph{KV cache reuse.}
Prefix caching reuses the KV of an identical prompt prefix~\citep{radixattention}. A writer reads its summaries in arbitrary order and subsets, so a shared prefix rarely exists; agent-aware systems work around this by fixing the prefix order~\citep{plato}. Position-independent reuse lifts the restriction: it blends cached KV into a new prompt and selectively recomputes a few tokens. CacheBlend picks those tokens by KV deviation~\citep{cacheblend}; KVCOMM aligns caches across agents with anchors~\citep{kvcomm}; DroidSpeak shares caches across models~\citep{droidspeak}; RelayCaching forwards decode-produced KV to downstream prefill~\citep{relaycaching}. All of them decide what to recompute from the cache side alone. None asks what the downstream call will attend to, and a token can deviate greatly yet draw no attention. Our criterion is the product of deviation and downstream attention, derived from a first-order bound on the output error.

\paragraph{Speculative decoding.}
Retrieval-based drafters propose tokens copied from a reference or from the context~\citep{llma,suffixdecoding}. Trained draft heads predict tokens from the target's hidden state~\citep{eagle3}. Adaptive control exists, but it acts within a call: SpecDec++ tunes the draft length from the drafts accepted so far~\citep{specdecpp}, and BanditSpec selects speculation hyperparameters online~\citep{banditspec}. Drafters have also been trained toward a target distribution, offline or on live traffic~\citep{distillspec,osd,aurora}. Agent deployments so far use heads as-is: a production commerce agent runs EAGLE-3 training-free and leaves domain fine-tuning to future work~\citep{paypal-eagle}. We differ on both counts. We route each call between a retrieval drafter and a trained head, using a copy rate predicted before generation begins, and we specialize the head on the agent's own trajectories.

\paragraph{Agent serving systems.}
Parrot exposes inter-request structure to the engine through semantic variables~\citep{parrot}, and Autellix schedules agent programs to reduce end-to-end latency~\citep{autellix}. These systems act at the scheduling layer: they decide when and where calls run. We act below them, cutting the cost of each call itself, and the two layers compose.
